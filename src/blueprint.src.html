Template · Power Platform target architecture

Power Platform Enterprise Blueprint

A reference architecture for running Power Apps, Power Automate and Copilot Studio in a global organisation. It covers how environments are laid out, how data policies stack, how capacity is paid for and charged back, and how the platform reports on itself. Each sheet shows a diagram first, then the detail behind it. Sheet 10 works through common governance scenarios, such as cross-charging and restricting the GitHub Copilot harness. Sheet 11 covers the operating loop: control, monitor, report and cross-charge. Sheet 12 ties business outcomes to the decisions, controls and measures, and pre-decides the response to each eventuality: Microsoft changes, organisation changes and incidents.

Act now · 15 November 2026

Unmanaged developer and Dataverse for Teams environments are retiring

From 15 Nov 2026 nobody can create an unmanaged developer environment or a Dataverse for Teams environment. Unmanaged developer environments are suspended from 15 Dec 2026 and deleted from 15 Jan 2027. Dataverse for Teams environments keep working until 15 Sep 2027, are then suspended, and are decommissioned on 30 Oct 2027 unless upgraded to Dataverse. Developer environments created by routing are managed and unaffected, and each user can hold one managed developer environment. List both types now (Manage › Environments, filter by type and last activity), convert or move what's still needed, and let the rest expire. Sheet 12 keeps the dates.

In effect

AI Builder credits stopped on 1 November 2026

Since 1 November 2026, AI Builder capacity add-ons are end of life and new or renewed premium licences carry no seeded AI Builder credits. Credits you already hold are honoured to the end of your contract term. After that, AI Builder features keep working but draw Copilot Credits, and fail if none are available. If you haven't already, inventory AI Builder use, plan Copilot Credits for the affected environments, and make sure auto-renew is off on AI Builder add-ons. Sheet 05 has the detail.

Plan now

Managed environments need premium licences, enforced from February 2027

Every user who runs an app or cloud flow in a managed environment needs a premium licence (Power Apps Premium, Power Automate Premium or a qualifying Dynamics 365 licence) or a pay-as-you-go meter. Microsoft has shown in-app warnings since June 2026. From February 2027, unlicensed users can't open apps in managed environments. This blueprint makes most environments managed (D4), so run the Users requiring licenses in Managed Environments report and budget for the result before you scale out.

Strategic intent

Outcomes the platform is steered by

  • Regions pay for what they use, with a statement they can audit.
  • Data stays in its zone: personal, business or restricted, never mixed.
  • Agents scale without surprise bills and without unowned identities.
  • Audit-ready at any time: every environment governed, every change traceable.
  • Makers are productive in minutes, inside the guardrails from the first minute.
  • Nothing depends on one person: automation runs as service principals, rights are just-in-time.

Sheet 12 traces each outcome to the decisions, controls and measures that deliver it.

Principles

Rules this blueprint doesn't trade

  • Set each control at the broadest scope that is right for it: tenant, then group, then environment.
  • The cost owner is the environment owner. Nothing is funded from a pool without a name on it.
  • Default to managed and governed. Every exception has an owner and an expiry date.
  • Prefer native Microsoft capability over custom tooling, and retire what Microsoft retires.
  • Every product fact on this page carries a source and the date it was checked.
  • Pre-decide the response to foreseeable change (sheet 12). Decide the rest at the next steering.

Decisions

Ten decisions this blueprint makes

D1 · TENANT SHAPE

One global tenant. Environment groups are cut by tier × region to mirror the operating regions.

D2 · DEFAULT ENV

The default environment becomes Personal Productivity: core Microsoft 365 and non-blockable connectors only, routing on, Share with Everyone off.

D3 · DATA ZONES

Three zones: G personal, A business, R restricted; every environment group belongs to exactly one zone. Allowlisting starts in Zone R.

D4 · MANAGED

Every environment except the default is managed, so group rules hold the settings. Dataverse for Teams environments are upgraded to Dataverse or left to expire (30 Oct 2027). The trade-off: users of managed environments need premium licences, enforced from Feb 2027.

D5 · RELEASE

Production accepts managed solutions only, deployed by Pipelines as a service principal. Zone R uses pro-dev CI/CD.

D6 · PAYING

A prepaid baseline is pooled and allocated per environment. Growth runs on pay-as-you-go through one billing policy per region × cost owner.

D7 · AI CREDITS

AI Builder credits ended on 1 Nov 2026 for new and renewed licences; existing ones run to contract end. Plan Copilot Credits for every AI Builder use.

D8 · TELEMETRY

Native inventory and data export feed a Fabric governance lakehouse. The CoE Starter Kit is not the base for new build.

D9 · AGENTS

Agents are identities: new ones get an Entra Agent ID. Add quarterly access reviews and zone-specific connector rules.

D10 · PEOPLE

A global Platform CoE, regional hubs and BU champions. Three maker tiers with promotion triggers read from inventory.

Assumptions to confirm

One Microsoft Entra tenant for all regions; tenant home geo in Europe; Microsoft 365 E3/E5 as the user baseline; sovereign-cloud jurisdictions (for example mainland China) handled separately, outside this design. Environment names and examples are illustrative.

Sheet 01 · Architecture

Reference architecture

Five layers. People configure a tenant-wide control plane, which enforces policy on environments grouped into zones. Environments reach data through a few governed paths, and every layer emits telemetry that returns to the people who run the platform.

Products on this sheet
Power PlatformPower AppsPower AutomateCopilot StudioDataverse

Why five layers

Each layer has one owner and one change process. The control plane changes through the CoE change board; zones change through group rule templates; integration paths change through the Cyber Defense Center. That keeps a change in one place from silently weakening another.

Where agents fit

Copilot Studio agents live in Zone A for read-only assistants and in Zone R as soon as they take actions on business systems. Their tools (connectors and MCP servers) are governed by the same data policies as apps and flows, and each agent has its own Entra identity.

Creation & clean-up

  • Creation is restricted. Only admins create production, sandbox and trial environments. Developer environments come only through routing, which creates managed ones, one per user; unmanaged developer environments can't be created after 15 Nov 2026 and are deleted from 15 Jan 2027. Everything else goes through an intake form.
  • Copilot Managed Runtime routing is always on (preview). Microsoft enables routing for Copilot Managed Runtime in every tenant and keeps a Microsoft-managed default environment group for it; neither can be removed. Because this blueprint routes only the licensed-maker security groups, makers outside them can't build Copilot Managed Runtime apps until you add them or create an Everyone rule, which must be last in priority. The Copilot Studio app-creation path itself can be turned off or scoped to security groups in the Microsoft 365 admin center (Set up app creation spaces).
  • Licence auto-claim is on. A user who opens a premium or managed-environment app gets a Power Apps licence, if one is free in the pool. Keep headroom in the pool, because unlicensed users are blocked in managed environments from February 2027.
  • The default environment gets cleaned out. Shared multi-maker assets move to shared dev, then production. Single-maker assets move to the owner's dev environment. Anything unused for 90 days goes to quarantine after the owner is told.

Status check

Microsoft Learn documents ACP as generally available per workload once design-time enforcement reaches that maker portal (Power Automate, then Copilot Studio, then Power Apps). Until then it is enforced at runtime only for that workload. Start in mixed mode (the default). Don't switch Zone R to ACP-only while custom, HTTP, desktop-flow and Copilot Studio virtual-connector controls still live in the classic layer 2 policy. Endpoint filtering is still in preview and doesn't evaluate values held in environment variables.

Sheet 04 · Application lifecycle

Release paths

Two paths share one rule: nothing unmanaged reaches production. Citizen and fusion teams use Pipelines in Power Platform from a host in their own geo. Zone R and Tier 3 solutions use a pro-dev path that builds the managed artifact from source and runs automated tests.

Products on this sheet
PipelinesPower AppsPower AutomateDataverse

Solution standards

  • Turn on Create new canvas apps and cloud flows in Dataverse solutions in every dev environment, and have each maker set a preferred solution, so new assets land in a solution.
  • Every endpoint, site URL and ID goes in an environment variable. Every connection goes through a connection reference owned by a service account, never a person. Endpoint filtering doesn't evaluate values held in environment variables, so for those endpoints the API gateway is the real control.
  • The solution checker is enforced by group rule: Block in every target (Block stops imports with critical issues only). Review high findings in the pull request for Zone R.

Source & identity

  • Dataverse Git integration (Azure DevOps, or GitHub in preview; requires managed environments) is on for shared dev and every Tier 2 or Tier 3 solution, so any version can be rebuilt.
  • One deployment service principal per geo, owned in Entra by the Platform CoE, with Deployment Pipeline Administrator in the pipelines host and System Administrator in target environments.
  • Pipelines targets must be managed environments. Since October 2026 Pipelines has warned admins about unmanaged targets, and deployments to them are blocked 30 days after the warning.
  • Approvals run as a flow in the Pipelines host, so every gate leaves an audit trail.

Sheet 05 · Credit consumption & FinOps

Paying for capacity

Capacity reaches environments by two routes. The prepaid route pools licences and capacity packs at tenant level and allocates them per environment. The metered route links environments through a billing policy to an Azure subscription. Both end in a chargeback to the region that owns the P&L.

Pay-as-you-go caveat

Linking an environment to a billing policy changes what it can use: Process licences, the unattended RPA add-on, app passes and Power Pages capacity are ignored there. Keep RPA and app-pass workloads in environments without a billing policy.

How do we charge each business unit for the Power Platform and Copilot Studio capacity it actually uses?

Steps

  1. Register a cost owner for every environment: environment ID, region, BU, cost centre and owner, kept in the governance environment. The naming convention already carries region and BU.
  2. Choose how each environment is funded. Use a prepaid allocation for predictable workloads and a pay-as-you-go billing policy for variable ones. Create one billing policy per cost owner per region, and tag its Azure subscription with the cost centre.
  3. Allocate Copilot Credits per environment and switch off "Draw from the available capacity in my tenant" so each environment's prepaid spend stops at its allocation. Enforce it at scale with the environment-group rule Cost controls – Draw from tenant credit pool.
  4. Set the tenant's add-on capacity assignment setting to "Only specific admins". If environment admins are allowed, they can allocate credits to every environment in the tenant, not just their own.
  5. Cut off on day 1 from the lakehouse's daily loads (sheet 06): Azure cost exports, the Copilot Studio consumption report, licence reports and inventory.
  6. Publish the statement per region, BU and cost centre. Review it at the FinOps board and settle disputes before the books close.
Watch out for

Common traps

  • An allocation alone isn't a boundary. If the environment can still draw from the tenant pool or has a billing policy, it keeps spending.
  • Azure budgets alert but don't stop usage. Where a workload needs a hard stop, add an agent monthly limit with Stop usage, with a margin: it isn't applied instantly and doesn't stop the agent flows the agent calls.
  • Prepaid credits are shared with Microsoft 365. Credits allocated to Power Platform environments reduce what Copilot Cowork and Work IQ can use. Agree the split with the Microsoft 365 admin.
  • Reports lag. Copilot Studio consumption data is aggregated daily and can be delayed, and Azure costs take up to 24 hours. Close the month from exported data, not dashboards.

How do we let finance makers use SAP and sales makers use Salesforce, without opening either connector to everyone?

This is the Phase 3 shape of Zone A (sheet 09). The Zone A non-production and production groups are split per connector pack, adding groups to the 26 on sheet 02. Sheet 03's Zone A column shows the union of all packs; each group gets only its own. Until then, Zone A runs on the classic policy from sheet 03.

Steps

  1. Define connector packs: a baseline every maker gets, plus add-on packs per business need, such as finance (SAP, SQL), sales (Salesforce) and engineering.
  2. Create one environment group per pack per region (for example EG-A-FIN-{R}). Give each an advanced connector policy equal to the baseline plus its pack. Use the Power Platform API to copy the baseline policy between groups so they stay in step.
  3. Restrict risky connectors by action. Set them to SomeAllowed and list only the permitted actions, such as read-only Salesforce or named SAP calls.
  4. Bind each environment to an Entra security group and give its members the maker role through a Dataverse group team.
  5. Publish an access package per pack in Entra ID Governance. Require approval from the BU champion, set a 180-day expiry and add a quarterly access review.
  6. Run in mixed mode and keep the classic policy. Watch for blocked attempts and fix the allowlist. ACP-only isn't an option while custom, HTTP and Copilot Studio virtual connectors are governed by the classic policy (sheet 03).
Watch out for

Common traps

  • Everyone in an environment gets all of its connectors. Don't mix audiences in one environment.
  • Policies stick. Each environment has one effective connector policy. If you move an environment out of a group or remove the group rule, the last policy stays in place until you remove it on the environment.
  • New connectors are blocked by default. Publish a request path so makers aren't stuck.
  • Custom, HTTP and MCP: keep the classic data policy for custom and HTTP connectors. MCP servers can only be allowed or blocked whole.

Steps

  1. Set the add-on capacity assignment tenant setting to "Only specific admins". Control who can author with environment security groups and Dataverse security roles (Environment Maker or a custom role).   2. Publish the rule Cost controls – Draw from tenant credit pool = off on Zone G and AI lab groups, and don't link a billing policy to them.
  3. Create AI lab groups per region. Allocate credits, then bind each environment to a security group and an access package.
  4. Tighten what lab agents can reach. In the classic data policy, block Knowledge source with public websites and data and unneeded channel connectors, and turn off Bing search on the environment. Use the group rules Knowledge sources for agents and Skills in Copilot Studio. In ACP, allow MCP servers by name only in Zone R.
  5. Automate daily: find agents with harness = GitHub Copilot in inventory, apply your development limit with Stop usage, and notify the owner. Block or quarantine agents that breach policy. Admin center has no tenant-wide default agent limit, and the inventory API needs a delegated admin account.

Watch out for

Common traps

  • Portal entry can't be blocked. Anyone can open the harness experience, with or without a Copilot Studio licence. What you control is whether they can save, publish, spend and reach data.
  • Building isn't free. Building, previewing, testing and evaluating all consume credits, before anything is published. An environment with no credits is the closest thing to a block.
  • Developer and trial environments are billed. They moved to usage-based billing on 1 Sep 2026, so routed personal dev environments need the zero-credit posture above.
  • Capacity controls hit everything in an environment: standard agents, agent flows, prompts and harness agents alike. Check before you change them.
  • The Harness column is admin-center only. Automation has to derive the harness from inventory fields until properties.harness is returned.
  • Apps are not agents. Copilot Studio's Create apps path (preview) runs on Copilot Managed Runtime, and that path does have a switch: turn it off, or scope it to security groups, in the Microsoft 365 admin center. Harness agents have no such switch.

Back-charge rules

  • Overage: credits consumed above allocation under an approved tenant-pool exception, or pay-as-you-go use that wasn't budgeted, are back-charged at the internal rate the following month.
  • Corrections: errors found after close are fixed by back-charge or credit in the next cycle, never by reopening a closed month.
  • Unowned spend: charged to the platform budget, and the environment goes to quarantine until it has an owner.
  • Disputes: raised by the regional hub in the dispute window, and decided by the FinOps board.

Maturity path

  1. Showback for two or three months, until regions trust the numbers.
  2. Cross-charge direct costs: pay-as-you-go and licences.
  3. Add prepaid credits and Dataverse at agreed internal rates.
  4. Add shared platform cost and back-charges with an allocation key agreed once a year.

Steps · moving an environment out

  1. Freeze changes: no deployments to the environment during the move window.
  2. Source admin submits Move environment with the destination tenant ID. The destination admin approves within 7 days.
  3. Upload the user-mapping file; validate that every mapped user is active in the destination.
  4. Begin the move and allow up to 24 hours. Cancel is available until it completes.
  5. In the destination: assign System Administrator, re-enable Managed Environments, rebuild security groups, connection references, HTTP trigger URLs, and turn child flows on before parent flows.
  6. In the source: delete the empty shell, remove the billing policy and allocations, update the inventory and the cost statement.
Watch out for

Common traps

  • Security groups don't migrate. An admin who wasn't in the mapping file sees an empty environment: a permissions issue, not data loss.
  • Environments with finance and operations apps move whole; the source keeps nothing.
  • Geo matters. Moves are within a geo; a cross-geo divestiture is a rebuild, not a move.
